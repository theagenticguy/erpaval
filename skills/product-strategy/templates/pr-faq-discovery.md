## External FAQ (the five customer questions)

1. **Who is the customer?**
   [Specific — time, place, situation. Not "everyone." Example: "Engineering managers at 50–500-person distributed teams who run 5+ cross-time-zone meetings per week."]

2. **What is the customer problem or opportunity?**
   [Specific pain, not vague need. Evidence inline.]

3. **What is the most important customer benefit?**
   [Single, clear, measurable. Not a list.]

4. **How do we know the customer needs or wants this?**
   [Cite validated research: interviews, usability tests, support tickets, competitor signal, prior-launch data. Not gut feel.]

5. **What does the customer experience look like?**
   [End-to-end, narrated from the customer's POV.]

### Additional external questions (objections a competitor would raise)

- **How much does it cost?** [Price, tier, trial terms.]
- **When is it available?** [GA date, regional rollout, prerequisites.]
- **How does it compare to [incumbent]?** [Honest comparison; name the incumbent; don't only list your wins.]
- **What if [common objection]?** [Acknowledge and address.]

---

## Internal FAQ

1. **TAM and opportunity size?** [Evidence, not wish.]
2. **Unit economics?** [Cost per customer, margin, breakeven.]
3. **Top three technical risks?** [Named, with mitigation plans.]
4. **Dependencies that must land first?** [Sequence-blocking items.]
5. **Kill criteria — what would cause us to stop?** [Real kill criteria, not platitudes. Example: "If beta users do not confirm a meeting within 5 minutes for 80% of attempts by end of Q2, we halt and reassess."]
6. **How do we know the customer wants this?** [Specific research: interviews with N customers, prior launch data, competitor feature adoption — not opinion.]
7. **Year-2 roadmap sketch?** [Not a commitment. A direction.]
